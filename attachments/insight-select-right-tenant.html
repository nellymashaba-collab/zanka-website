How to Select the Right Tenant

Screening approaches that reduce risk and vacancies.

The right tenant is rarely the first applicant, and it’s rarely the one offering to pay the most upfront. A disciplined, consistent screening process is what actually reduces arrears and turnover risk, not gut instinct.

Start with affordability, not just income

A common rule of thumb is that rent shouldn’t exceed roughly 30% of gross monthly income, but income alone isn’t the full picture. Ask for at least three months of bank statements to see actual spending patterns, existing debt obligations and whether income is consistent or irregular.

Run a proper credit and affordability check

A credit check reveals payment history on existing accounts, judgments and overall debt exposure, information that a payslip alone won’t show. This should be a standard, non-negotiable step for every applicant, regardless of how strong an impression they make in person.

Verify employment directly

Where possible, confirm employment and salary directly with the employer rather than relying solely on a payslip, which is the easiest document to falsify. A short call to HR is a low-effort, high-value verification step.

Speak to a previous landlord, not just a personal reference

A previous landlord reference is far more predictive than a friend or family reference. Ask specifically about payment punctuality, property care and whether the tenancy ended on good terms, and be alert if an applicant can only provide a reference from many years ago.

Trust the pattern, not the exception

One late payment in a credit history isn’t automatically disqualifying, but a consistent pattern of late payments, multiple accounts in default, or an inability to provide standard documentation are signals worth taking seriously. Screening isn’t about finding a perfect applicant. It’s about avoiding a pattern that’s likely to repeat in your property.
