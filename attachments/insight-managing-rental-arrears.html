Managing Rental Arrears Effectively

A fair, firm process for handling late payments.

Arrears are one of the most stressful parts of being a landlord, and the instinct is often either to ignore a first late payment or to escalate immediately. Neither extreme serves the owner well. What works is a consistent, documented process applied the same way every time.

Act on day one, not day thirty

A polite, prompt reminder on the day rent is due but unpaid is far more effective than waiting two weeks to raise it. Most late payments are genuine oversights or short-term cash flow issues, and early, low-friction contact resolves the majority of them quickly.

Keep every communication in writing

Even if the first contact is a phone call, follow up with a written message summarising what was agreed. This protects both parties and becomes essential documentation if the matter ever needs to escalate further.

Use a consistent escalation process

  • Day 1–3: Friendly reminder, in writing
  • Day 7: Formal notice referencing the lease terms
  • Day 14–21: Written notice of intent to escalate if unresolved, with a clear final date
  • Beyond 21 days unresolved: Engage a debt collection process or attorney, per the lease terms

Applying the same timeline every time, rather than deciding case by case, removes ambiguity for both landlord and tenant, and protects the owner if the matter ever needs to go to the Rental Housing Tribunal or court.

Understand what you can and can’t do

South African law does not permit landlords to lock a tenant out, remove their belongings, or cut services to force payment. These actions expose the landlord to legal risk regardless of how far in arrears the tenant is. Formal eviction requires a court process; understanding this upfront prevents costly missteps under pressure.

Document everything for the eventual worst case

Even when a matter resolves amicably, keep every notice, payment record and communication on file. If a pattern repeats with the same tenant or the matter does escalate, a clean paper trail is the single biggest asset a landlord can have.
