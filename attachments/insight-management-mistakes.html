5 Common Property Management Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid costly mistakes and protect your investment.

Most costly property mistakes aren’t dramatic. They’re small process gaps that compound quietly over a year or two. These five come up most often.

1. Skipping a proper tenant screening process

Approving a tenant quickly to avoid vacancy is tempting, but a rushed screening is the single biggest predictor of arrears and disputes down the line. A consistent process, including a credit check, affordability check and previous landlord reference, costs a few days upfront and saves months of pain later.

2. No written record of the property’s condition

Without a signed, dated, photographed inspection report at move-in, deposit disputes at move-out become a matter of opinion rather than evidence. This is the single most preventable source of landlord-tenant conflict.

3. Reactive rather than scheduled maintenance

Waiting for something to break is more expensive than maintaining it. A geyser serviced annually rarely bursts; one left untouched for five years often does, usually at the worst possible time and with water damage attached.

4. Inconsistent rent reviews

Leaving rent untouched for multiple years to avoid an awkward conversation quietly erodes returns against inflation and rising costs. Smaller, regular, well-communicated increases are easier for tenants to absorb than one large catch-up increase.

5. Mixing personal and rental finances

Running rental income through a personal account makes it difficult to track true profitability, complicates tax filing, and often disguises properties that are quietly underperforming. A dedicated account per property, or at minimum per portfolio, makes the numbers honest.

Each of these is a process fix, not a large investment, which is exactly why they’re worth getting right from day one rather than after something has already gone wrong.
